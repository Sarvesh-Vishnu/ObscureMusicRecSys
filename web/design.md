# Obscure — design contract

Obscure is the web face of ObscureMusicRecSys: pick a song you know and see a **route** of five steps
to a song almost nobody plays, each step a move real ListenBrainz listeners made. A second view, the Lab,
shows the benchmark behind it. Readers: hiring managers and engineers judging recommender-system work.

**The one idea:** the route is drawn on a listeners axis, so a visitor *sees* it descend from the head
into the long tail, and every step carries its evidence (how many people made that move, how the
sound changed, whether someone made it again after the cutoff). One device carries it: the amber line.

## 1. Theme and atmosphere
- **Night line.** A player at night: near-black ground, warm ink. Rules out light mode and warm paper.
- **Evidence-first.** Every number is computed from the exported data; nothing typed. Rules out
  decorative stats, count-ups, invented axes.
- **Quiet.** One accent, hairlines, no cards, no glow, nothing loops.

## 2. Colour and roles
| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `--ground` | `#0d0e10` | page |
| `--raise` | `#15171a` | player bar, art placeholder, search field |
| `--tail` | `#111316` | long-tail zone in the route figure |
| `--rule` / `--rule-2` | `#26292e` / `#3a3e45` | hairlines / control borders |
| `--ink` | `#ece8e1` | primary text (15.8:1 on ground) |
| `--ink-2` | `#b4aea5` | secondary text (8.8:1) |
| `--ink-3` | `#8a857d` | labels, axes (5.3:1 ground, 4.9:1 raise: AA) |
| `--accent` | `#f0a63a` | **the route, and only the route** (9.4:1) |
| `--focus` | `#f6c77d` | keyboard focus ring (a lighter amber so focus never reads as a route) |
| hover on accent | `#f5b65a` | "Play the route" hover only |

Amber means "route": the route line and stops, the selected step number, "Play the route", the
highlighted series in a Lab figure (the thing that figure is about). Nothing else is amber.
Dark only, by design (Night line). Palette validator: accent vs neutral passes CVD separation
(ΔE 17.6) and contrast; its lightness/chroma checks fail because the neutral is meant to read as
grey (a highlight encoding, not a categorical palette).

## 3. Typography
- Instrument Sans Variable (display and body; weights 400/500/600) and JetBrains Mono Variable
  (labels, axes, figures, numbers). Self-hosted via @fontsource; no Google requests.
- Scale: 11–12.5 mono labels · 14 small · 16 body · 17–18 lede · 18 side heads · 24–32 chapter heads ·
  32–58 page heads (clamp).
- Tabular numerals wherever numbers sit in columns. Measure ≤ 62ch.

## 4. Components
- **Route figure** (`RouteFigure.tsx`): x = step, y = listeners (log). Dashed head/tail threshold
  (from the export, not typed), tinted tail zone, amber line, stops (seed = ink, end = amber fill).
  Tweens 320 ms on dial change; instant with reduced motion. Drawn at the container's pixel width
  so text keeps its size on phones. Accessible name lists every stop; the tracklist is the
  keyboard/screen-reader equivalent.
- **Dials**: two radio groups as pill segments (λ smoothness, γ pull), three stops each, matching
  the precomputed grid exactly. Never a continuous slider: the routes are not interpolated.
- **Tracklist**: number, art (or empty frame), title/artist button that expands the evidence,
  listeners + head/tail, play (or MusicBrainz link when no preview). Evidence is a definition list.
- **Player bar**: appears on first play. Art, title, position in queue, prev/play/next, Apple credit
  link, 2 px amber progress. Previews cross over 1.6 s (equal power); a skip fades out in 0.25 s.
- **Lab figures** (`charts.tsx`): scatter with CI crosshairs, bars with CIs. Direct labels, hover
  and focus tooltips, "Show as table" under each.

## 5. Layout
- Max 1160, gutter 16 / 32. Route view: 7fr main (head, figure, tracklist) + 4fr side (dials,
  standard recommender, seed picker) at ≥ 1024; stacked below in that reading order.
- Lab: chapters as 4fr prose + 7fr figure at ≥ 1024; stacked below.

## 6. Depth
Rules and tone only. No shadows.

## 7. Do and don't
- Do round only the controls (buttons, segments, search): the one nod to a music player.
  Everything else is square. (`tells-ok: big-radius` in styles.css.)
- Don't type a number into copy. Sentences quoting numbers are built in `format.ts` or from
  `lab.json`; claims of "tied" or "no measurable cost" are worded from whether a paired 95% interval
  spans zero.
- Don't add a second accent, a light theme, or a gradient.
- Don't present the app's pull (closeness to the seed) as the benchmark's γ (closeness to a listener).

## 8. Responsive behaviour
- < 520 px: figure height 250, artist tag truncates at 18 chars, tracklist hides the listeners column
  (still in the evidence), evidence aligns full-width.
- Checked at 320, 375 and 1280: no horizontal scroll.

## 9. Notes for an agent
- Data: `public/data/{routes,tracks,lab}.json` are generated by `scripts/export_web.py` and
  `scripts/fetch_previews.py` (repo root). Don't edit by hand. Don't run the export while
  fetch_previews is running (it rewrites tracks.json at the end).
- `npm run dev` (port 5192), `npm test` (vitest: checks the exported routes keep the benchmark's
  rules), `npm run build`. Design scan: `node ~/.claude/skills/munnetrum-design/scripts/tells.mjs web`.

## 10. Amendments

### 10.1 — First build (2026-11-17)
Night line direction, two views (Route, Lab), iTunes previews, name "Obscure" (all chosen by the user).

**Considered and rejected.** Liner-notes (light, tracklist-as-footnotes) and Lab-bench (graph
inspector) directions; one long page; links-only audio; running beam search in the browser (the
subgraph a beam can touch is unbounded for continuous dials, so the dials snap to a precomputed grid
computed by the benchmarked code instead).

**Landing seed.** Chosen by a rule, not by hand: among songs with 500+ listeners whose default
route ends in the long tail, the steadiest descent, then most steps repeated after the cutoff.

**Lead artist.** The benchmark keys artists by credit, so "Korn feat. Skrillex" ≠ "Korn"; a route
could visit Korn four times. The app's routes use the lead artist; the Lab says so.

### 10.2 — Lab chapters 6 and 7: off-policy evaluation and bandits (2026-11-17)
*Changes: §4 components (two figure types).*

Two figures join the Lab. **Intervals**: one row per target policy, the true value as an ink tick,
the doubly robust 90% range in amber (the estimator the chapter is about) and the direct method's in
grey beneath it, effective sample size at the right; rows sorted by it, so the bands widen down the
page. **Lines**: running hit rate over rounds on a log x axis, LinUCB in amber, the hindsight fit as
a dashed reference, labels at the line ends nudged apart.

Every comparative sentence is conditional on the data: "cannot say which is better" only when one
policy's DR range contains the other's true value; "still larger than the gap" from the numbers.

**Considered and rejected.** An RMSE-vs-log-size line chart (four estimators × seven policies is
28 lines; the table has it). Plotting ε-greedy and Thompson curves (they sit on top of LinUCB and
the greedy learner; the table has them).

**Check.** Desktop and 375 px: no horizontal scroll; tests pass.

### 10.3 — Lead artist, fixed (2026-11-17)
*Amends: 10.1 "Lead artist".*

The first rule missed "×", "•", "w/" and "reinterpreted by" (so Wonderwall's route visited
Linkin Park twice), and split "ft" without spaces, cutting "Daft Punk" to "da" and "Taylor
Swift" to "taylor swi". Punctuation now splits anywhere; separator words split only with
whitespace on both sides. Same rule in `export_web.py` and `data.ts`, with tests on both
sides. Re-export changed 4 of 1,197 routes and no seeds.

### 10.4 — Playback that works everywhere, covers on every list, accessibility pass (2026-11-17)
*Changes: §4 components (player, seed head, side lists).*

**Playback.** Safari and iOS start audio only from inside the user's own gesture; the first player
called `play()` after a React render, so taps did nothing there. Every control now sets the source
and plays synchronously in its handler; the element then continues on its own between previews. A
preview that fails says so in the bar and the queue moves on. The OS media controls (lock screen,
headset, keyboard media keys) get title, artist, artwork and play/pause/next/previous.

**Covers.** The seed has its album cover (168 px; 112 px on phones). The standard recommender's
five songs and the seed picker carry covers, and those five are playable. A song with no matched
preview keeps an empty frame and a MusicBrainz link, never a stand-in picture.

**Accessibility** (axe-core 4.10 on both views: 0 violations, from 2 serious types on 45 nodes).
Chart points carry `role="img"` with their label; scrolling tables are focusable named regions; the
skip link shows on focus and moves focus without touching the hash route; the route summary is a
polite live region, so a dial change is announced; the tracklist and dials have headings; play
buttons name the artist as well as the song.

**Considered and rejected.** Playing the "other ways on" songs (they were never matched to previews;
matching them costs ~2 hours of lookups for a secondary list). Autoplay on load (needs a gesture
anyway, and sound nobody asked for).

### 10.5 — The figure follows the music; standard picks on the figure; fewer silent stops (2026-11-18)
*Changes: §4 components (route figure, tracklist, seed picker).*

**Playback on the figure.** The stop whose preview is loaded gets an amber ring and the figure's
label; while the route itself is the queue, the stretch not yet heard is drawn faint and dotted,
so the descent fills in as you listen. The tracklist row of the playing stop turns amber
(`aria-current`). Selection (the halo and the open evidence) stays the reader's; playback never
expands or collapses a row, so nothing shifts under the reader.

**Standard picks.** The ItemKNN top 5 sit in a grey column of their own past step 5, headed
"standard", with a key line under the caption counting how many are in the head. Past the end, not
at step 1: the comparison that matters is where each leaves you, and at step 1 a route that starts
in the head (Take On Me → Toto) made the cluster read as "the same".

**Fewer silent stops.** Apple's search leaves many best-known recordings out of its results
("Green Day American Idiot" returns covers). `fetch_previews.py --fallback` retries each miss
through the artist's catalogue (exact lead-artist name, then the same matcher), US store then
Indian. A route with gaps says how many of its songs have a preview, the picker marks seeds that
are not fully playable, and the landing rule prefers a route you can hear end to end.

**Considered and rejected.** Grey dots at step 1 (above). Auto-opening the playing stop's evidence
(it shifts the page every 30 seconds). A dot travelling between stops with the preview's progress
(the stop is what's playing, not the move). Loosening the matcher to fill gaps (a wrong song is
worse than a link to MusicBrainz).

### 10.6 — Crossfades (2026-11-18)
*Changes: §4 player.*

Two audio elements take turns. With 1.6 s of the playing preview left, the next starts on the
other element and the two cross over with equal-power curves (cos/sin, so the overlap doesn't dip
or swell); the queue position, figure ring and tracklist move at the start of the crossover. A
skip, previous or another song fades the old preview out over 0.25 s instead of clicking off;
pausing mid-crossover stops both. The idle element is started once, silently, inside the first
tap, so a browser that wants a gesture per element lets it play later; if one still refuses, the
player drops back to one element and cuts for the rest of the visit.

**iPhone and iPad keep cuts.** iOS Safari ignores an audio element's volume (the player detects
this), so a fade there would need Web Audio. Apple's preview server allows it (CORS `*`), but
routing previews through Web Audio on iOS risks silence when the screen locks, and it can't be
checked without a device. Revisit after a real-device test.

**Considered and rejected.** Web Audio everywhere (above). Longer crossfades (3 s+ eats a tenth of
a 30-second preview, and the step being judged is the sound of the next song, not the blend).
A setting to turn fades off (nobody asked; a skip is already near-instant).

### 10.7 — The Lab opens with what it found (2026-11-18)
*Changes: §4 components (Lab head); replaces the chapter strip.*

Under the dataset facts, "What it found": one row per chapter, numbered, the chapter's title as the
link and one sentence with its headline numbers. Titles come from one `TITLE` map shared with the
chapters; every number and every comparative word ("tie", "cannot tell it from", "does as well") is
computed from `lab.json` the same way the chapter computes it. A render test (`LabView.test.tsx`)
fails if a finding loses its link target or its number, or if anything prints NaN or undefined.
It ends with a link to the limits. Readers: a hiring manager who reads only the top of the page.

**Considered and rejected.** Keeping the mono chapter strip beside the list (two tables of
contents). Big stat tiles per finding (decorative, and the numbers need their comparison to mean
anything). Copying the README's six findings verbatim (typed numbers; and the Lab has seven chapters).

### 10.8 — Another route, sample length, play from a stop, About (2026-11-19)
*Changes: §4 components (dials, route figure, player, seed picker); a third view. Amends 10.6 (fade lengths).*

**Another route** (the user's pick for "a route can miss"). `export_web.py` runs the same
benchmarked `find_route` again with every stop of the earlier routes blocked, twice: three routes
per seed and setting that share no stop. They live in `routes-more.json`, loaded after the page, so
first load is unchanged. A third dial, "Route: Best · 2nd · 3rd", appears once they load; the URL
carries it (`r=2`). Every seed and setting got both alternatives; at the default setting 244 of 266
alternatives end in the long tail (the rest say "still in the head" like any route).

**The music follows the page.** Moving a dial or changing route while the route plays carries the
music to the same step of the new route (the same song carries on uninterrupted). Picking a seed
while anything plays plays the new route; picking never scrolls the page any more (it used to jump
to the top), and each seed has its own play button.

**Play from a stop.** Each stop on the figure is a button: click, tap or Enter plays the route from
there (crossfading), and clicking the stop that's playing pauses it. The figure is now `role=group`
with named buttons, not one `role=img`.

**Sample length** 10 / 15 / 30 s (default 30, remembered per browser). Crossfades are longer and
gentler than 10.6: 4 s at 30 s, 3 s at 15 s, 2 s at 10 s, on raised-cosine curves (flat at both
ends) instead of equal power, because the user heard 10.6's 1.6 s as abrupt. Skips cross both ways
in 0.7 s. The last song of a route fades out at the sample's end.

**About** (`#/about`): what Obscure is for (the long tail, not sound-alikes), why the steps are
real, how it handles a miss, how it's built. Copy is a draft in Claude's words for the author to
edit; its numbers are computed, and it says the default routes all end in the tail because seeds
without such a route were left out.

**Considered and rejected.** Beam runners-up as alternatives (they share four of five stops).
"Swap a stop" and "not for me" (offered; the user chose another route). Web Audio crossfades on iOS
(still the 10.6 risk). Auto-scroll on seed pick (the user found it disorienting).

### 10.9 — The end card (2026-11-19)
*Changes: §4 components (a new end card on the player). Unpublished until the author says.*

When a route's last sample finishes, a card docks on the player: **where the route ended**
(title, artist, cover), its listeners and how many times fewer than the seed (named), and links to
Apple Music and MusicBrainz. Three ways on: **Try the 2nd route** (amber: it plays a route; it
starts at step 1, since every route shares the seed you just heard; after the last route it reads
"Back to the best route"), **Play again** (from the seed) and **Another song** (focuses the seed
search). It closes with × or Escape, and goes away once anything plays.

The player now knows when a queue has finished (`ended`): the last song's fade-out completing, its
cut, or its `ended` event. A sample resumed past its end now stops at the end, instead of
playing on to the end of the preview.

On phones the card drops its cover (the bar just below shows the same one) and the buttons wrap
under the text; it never scrolls sideways at 320. The finish is announced through a live region
that is always mounted (one that mounts already filled is often not read). Figure stops now say
"Pause" only while the music is actually playing.

Also fixed: with "About" added, the top nav was 12px too wide at 320. It tightens below 360.

**Considered and rejected.** Opening the card as a modal (it would interrupt someone reading the
stops). Auto-playing the next route (a miss should cost one click, not happen to you). Repeating
"k of 5 stops in the long tail" (the figure caption already says it). Moving focus to the card
(the listener may be anywhere on the page; the live region tells a screen reader instead).
